# Legendary Card Competition — Concept Document

_Converted from `Concept Document.docx` (project files). The .docx is the source of truth; re-convert if it changes._

## One-liner

Legendary Card Competition is web-based game designed to offer struggling readers with an engaging means of practicing and learning foundational literacy skills through a card battling game that resembles Slay the Spire and Yu-gi-oh!

## The Core Belief

Evidence-based reading activities can be translated into meaningful and fun game mechanics with fidelity. The core game mechanics will resemble and serve the same purpose as specified evidence-based reading exercises. The game mechanics will keep struggling readers engaged in gameplay and learning beyond the capacity of traditional forms of reading instruction.

## The Concept

The game is designed as a “card battler” similar to Yu-Gi-Oh! and Slay the Spire. The player will learn how to use magical cards with magical effects in a combat arena. The player will collect cards by defeating opponents (other card players), explicit lessons (via NPC tutorials), and training sessions (battling monsters and training figures). Players will progress from basic trainer cards that teach students proper letter formation and letter names, to basic cards that teach students letter/sound associations, to combo cards that can be combined to make words, to elite cards that contain words, and to legendary cards with contain complete thoughts/concepts/clauses.

The cards effects are triggered in competition through multi-sensory reading activities (i.e., letter forming/tracing, letter naming, speaking letter sounds, reading/spelling words). The main game loop will involve taking an explicit lesson a library from a sensei/tutor figure, training with a new card (as needed), then creating a deck from collected cards, and then entering a card competition. In each of stage of the main loop, there will be opportunities to explicitly each and practice literacy concepts with the cards. The final loop of a game competition serves as a formative assessment for the lessons learned (the cards used).

## Game Phases

### Phase 1 – Tutorial and Trainer Cards

- Skills
  - Orthographic knowledge: letter identification, letter formation, letting naming
  - Phonological knowledge: sound discrimination, phoneme blending, phoneme segmenting
  - Phonics: letter/sound association, sound/letter association
- Content
  - Letter symbols: upper- and lower-case letters
  - Phonemes: consonant phonemes, short and long vowels
- Game Loop
  - Explicit lesson at library
    - Tutor/Sensei NPC teaches player a new letter and gives a new card
  - Practice in training rooms
    - Player takes cards to training rooms to engage in mini-games to practice with owned cards
  - Deck building at library
    - Player selects from card storage which cards will be taken into the competition
    - When adding card to deck, player must show mastery of card to use in competition
  - Card competition
    - Player challenges an opponent at the card tables (like Gym Leaders in Pokemon or progressively higher levels of duelists as in Yu-Gi-Oh!).
    - Battle sequence
      - Turn 0: Both players draw 5 cards and flip a coin to determine who starts
      - Turn 1 Options (choose 1)
        - Attack by selecting card and activating card
          - Select card by touching card and tracing the letter
          - Activate card by naming card
        - Discard (up to 5) cards and draw as many cards as discarded.
      - Turn 2 Options (choose 1)
        - Attack by selecting card and activating card
          - Select card by touching card and tracing the letter
          - Activate card by naming card
        - Discard (up to 5) cards and draw as many cards as discarded.
      - Turn 3 Options and beyond (same as Turn 2)
        - Combat competitions should roughly last 5 turns.
    - Battle Components
      - When card a is activated, then it emits a burst of energy equal to the damage points on the cards.
      - The energy is aimed at a character avatar representing an opponent. It is accompanied by a health bar that decreases as damage is taken.

### Phase 2 – Solo Battles and Basic Cards

- Skills
  - Orthographic knowledge: letter tracing, letter naming
  - Phonological knowledge: phoneme blending, phoneme segmenting
  - Phonics: letter/sound association, sound/letter association
- Content
  - Letter symbols: lower-case letters, consonant digraphs, vowel digraphs
  - Phonemes: all phonemes corresponding to letter symbols
- Game Loop
  - Explicit lesson at library
    - Tutor/Sensei NPC teaches player a new letter and gives a new card.
      - New cards at this stage are “Basic Cards”. They deal more damage than trainer cards but require players to speak the sound of the card. Some Basic Cards contain bonus effects like in the game Uno, such as causing the opponent to skip a turn or causing the opponent to discard a card.
  - Practice in training rooms
    - Player takes cards to training rooms to engage in mini-games to practice with owned cards.
  - Deck building at library
    - Player selects from card storage which cards will be taken into the competition.
    - When adding card to deck, player must show mastery of card to use in competition.
  - Card competition
    - Player challenges an opponent at the card tables as in Phase 1.
    - Battle sequence
      - Turn 0: Both players draw 5 cards, both players receive a deck of 5 cue cards and flip a coin to determine who starts. The first cue card for each player is revealed to the player.
        - Cue cards provide guidance as to which cards can be played for bonus points. For example, a cue card may contain a partially complete word and a picture (_ a t, cat picture). If the player plays the letter that fits the cue card, then they get bonus points.
      - Turn 1 Options (choose 1)
        - Attack by selecting, placing, and activating card
          - Select card by touching card and tracing the letter
          - Place the card by naming letter on the card
          - Speak the sound of the card to blow energy through the card and attack opponent.
        - Discard (up to 5) cards and draw as many cards as discarded.
      - Turn 2 Options (choose 1)
        - Defend card and attempt to steal card
          - This option will appear before the adversary names the card. To defend and steal the card, the player will speak the sound of the card. The opponent will then take the next turn.
        - Attack by selecting card and activating card
          - Select card by touching card and tracing the letter
          - Activate card by naming card
        - Discard (up to 5) cards and draw as many cards as discarded.
      - Turn 3 Options and beyond (same as Turn 2)
        - Combat competitions should roughly last 5-8 turns.
    - Battle Components
      - When card a is activated, then it emits a burst of energy equal to the damage points on the cards.
      - The energy is aimed at a character avatar representing an opponent. It is accompanied by a health bar that decreases as damage is taken.
